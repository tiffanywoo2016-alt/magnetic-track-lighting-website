Spot, Flood, Linear Flood, or Grille — A Practical Guide to Choosing the Right Beam Angle for Your Magnetic Track Lighting Project

By Tiffany Woo • June 22, 2026 • 5 min read

Most buyers choose the wrong beam angle because they pick a module by appearance before they know what the light needs to do. A spotlight looks elegant in a showroom, but put it in a corridor and you get glare complaints. A flood light feels safe, but aim it at artwork and the accent is gone.

The beam angle is not a cosmetic choice — it is the single most important technical decision when choosing a magnetic track module for your project. Whether you are specifying for a retail track lighting installation, a hotel corridor, or an open-plan office, getting the beam wrong means the lighting effect fails regardless of the fixture quality. Here is a practical guide for importers, lighting designers and contractors who need to make the right call from the start.

Quick Answer

Spotlight — accent lighting for retail displays, artwork and reception desks where direction and contrast matter. If you are comparing a spotlight vs flood for commercial track lighting, this is the key distinction: spotlights create focus, floods create ambience.

Flood / Linear Flood — even ambient light for corridors, offices and wall washing.

Grille module — anti-glare control for reading areas, hotel corridors and classrooms where people spend extended time under direct light.

Pendant — decorative hanging installation for dining tables, bar counters and lounge zones.

Magnetic Track Lighting Beam Angles: How to Choose the Right Module for Your Commercial Project

Retail display or window — Spotlight

Tight beam (15° to 30°) creates contrast between product and background, drawing the customer’s eye. For any retail track lighting project, this is the go-to choice. Avoid flood in this role — it washes out the product and removes the visual hierarchy a display needs.

Gallery artwork — Spotlight 15°–24°

Precise accent with minimal light spill onto adjacent pieces. Head angle is critical here — aim a 15° spotlight 30° off-target and the beam misses the artwork entirely. Always account for swivel range when positioning spotlights above a gallery wall.

Open office or meeting room — Flood 60°–120°

Even general illumination with soft falloff. No hot spots, no harsh shadows. This is a pure ambient role — spotlights create distracting contrast in a work environment. For a low voltage track lighting system operating at 48V track power, flood modules deliver the most natural office-grade light distribution.

Hotel corridor or passageway — Grille module

Guests naturally look up while walking through a corridor. A standard flood creates direct glare at these viewing angles. For any hotel track lighting installation where guest comfort matters, a grille module uses a micro-louvre structure to cut glare above 45°, making the space comfortable to walk through. Trade-off: grille modules output 10–15% fewer lumens than a comparable open flood.

Wall washing or shelf lighting — Linear flood

Rectangular beam pattern washes a wall or shelf from end to end evenly. A round spotlight or standard flood creates scalloped pools of light — linear flood is the only module type that gives a continuous clean wash across a long surface. This makes it the preferred choice for retail wall displays, library shelving and feature walls in hotel lobbies.

Restaurant dining table — Spotlight or Pendant

Spotlight for focused table illumination that separates the dining zone from the ambient space. Pendant for a decorative presence at a lower mounting height — especially effective over island counters or centre tables.

Reading area, library or classroom — Grille module

Low UGR for extended visual comfort. People in these spaces will look up at the ceiling — a flood module causes eye fatigue over time. Grille is the correct choice wherever occupants stay in the same zone for more than 30 minutes.

Reception desk or counter — Spotlight

Directional accent aimed at the desk surface creates a professional, intentional look. Mount one spotlight per desk zone — two spotlights on the same counter create competing highlights that confuse the eye.

FAQ: Beam Angle Selection for Magnetic Track Lighting

Can I mix spot, flood and grille modules on the same track?
Yes. One magnetic track circuit can carry any combination of module types — accent spots for display zones, floods for ambient areas, grille modules for corridor sections — all on the same 48V rail. This modularity is the main advantage of a magnetic track system over fixed linear lighting.
Does a grille module reduce brightness significantly?
A grille module outputs approximately 10–15% fewer lumens than an open flood of the same wattage and LED chip. This is the physical result of the micro-louvre structure. The trade-off is significantly improved visual comfort. Always account for this reduction when calculating total module count for a space.
How do I decide between a round flood and a linear flood?
Use round flood when the light needs to cover a compact area — a meeting table, a corridor junction, a lounge seating zone. Use linear flood when the target surface is long and narrow — a wall, a shelf row, a reception counter. Linear flood produces a rectangular beam; round flood produces a circular one. Match the beam shape to the surface shape.
