Same Track, Different Modules — Why Some Cost More and What You're Actually Paying For

By Tiffany Woo • June 9, 2026 • 6 min read
The internal driver is the single most important differentiator between a basic module and a premium one.

If you've ever compared magnetic track lighting modules from different suppliers, you've probably noticed something puzzling: two modules that look almost identical and fit the exact same track can differ in price by 2x or even 3x.

Is it just markup? Brand premium? Or is there something real inside that justifies the difference?

The answer — more often than not — is hiding inside the module itself. It's the LED driver, and understanding what it does (or doesn't do) is the key to making smart procurement decisions for your projects.

The Short Answer: Not All Modules Have a Driver Inside

Here's the most important thing to know: some magnetic track modules come with a built-in driver, and some don't.

Modules without a built-in driver are often called "dumb modules" or "raw LED boards." They contain LEDs mounted on an aluminum PCB, but the current regulation happens elsewhere — typically in a central power supply that feeds multiple modules on the same track.

Modules with a built-in driver have a small, dedicated driver circuit inside the module housing. This driver converts the 48V track power into precise constant current for the specific LED chips inside that module.

Why Does a Built-in Driver Matter?

For a B2B buyer specifying lighting for commercial projects, the built-in driver makes a real difference in three areas:

WITH BUILT-IN DRIVER

Premium Modules

  • Each module operates at its optimal current
  • Consistent brightness regardless of how many modules share the track
  • Smooth, flicker-free dimming (0-10V / DALI / TRIAC compatible)
  • Individual module — if one fails, others stay on
  • 50,000+ hour driver lifespan
NO INTERNAL DRIVER

Basic Modules

  • Relies on an external central power supply
  • Current varies with cable length and module count
  • No or limited dimming capability
  • If the central supply fails, the entire track goes dark
  • Lower upfront cost, but hidden risks

The B2B Buyer's Takeaway

When you're comparing quotes from different suppliers, a 2x price difference on magnetic track modules is not automatically a red flag. What matters is understanding what's inside.

Here's a simple mental model:

Ask yourself: does this module have a built-in driver, or does it need an external one?

If the supplier can't clearly answer this question, that's a warning sign. A quality supplier should be able to tell you the driver brand, the constant current rating, the dimming protocol support, and the safety certifications — without hesitation.
