Built-in Driver vs. No Driver: What's the Difference in Magnetic Track Modules?

By Tiffany Woo • June 9, 2026 • 5 min read
The LED driver inside the module is the key difference between a basic and a premium magnetic track light.

You're looking at two magnetic track modules. They look almost identical. They clip into the same track. They use the same 48V power supply.

Yet one costs nearly twice as much as the other. Why?

The answer is simple: one has a built-in LED driver, and the other doesn't.

This isn't a minor spec difference — it fundamentally changes how the module behaves in a real project. In this article, we'll break down exactly what changes when a module has an internal driver versus when it doesn't.

What Is a "Driver" in a Magnetic Track Module?

An LED driver is a device that regulates the power going to the LED chips. LEDs need a constant current to operate correctly — too little and they flicker or dim unevenly, too much and they overheat and fail prematurely.

In a magnetic track system:

  • With built-in driver: Each module has its own dedicated driver inside the housing. It receives 48V from the track and outputs precise constant current to its own LED chips. Think of it as each module being self-contained — plug and play.
  • Without built-in driver: The module is essentially a bare LED board. It relies on the track's central power supply to deliver the right current. The module itself has no intelligence or regulation — it's a "dumb" light source.
Modules with built-in drivers install like any other magnetic track light — no extra wiring, no external driver boxes.

4 Things That Change

Here's a side-by-side comparison of what you get — and what you lose — with each type:

WITH BUILT-IN DRIVER

What You Get

  • Stable brightness — each module runs at its rated current, regardless of how many modules share the same track
  • Smooth dimming — compatible with 0-10V, DALI, or TRIAC dimming
  • Flicker-free — ripple < 1%, safe for cameras and sensitive eyes
  • Module-level redundancy — one module fails, others keep working
NO INTERNAL DRIVER

What You Lose

  • Uneven brightness — current varies based on cable length and load count
  • Limited or no dimming — most basic modules don't dim at all
  • Visible flicker — common with low-cost central power supplies
  • Single point of failure — if the central supply dies, the whole track goes dark

How to Choose: It Depends on Your Market Position

Whether you need modules with built-in drivers or not is not determined by the project type — it's determined by your customer's priorities and budget.

Choose modules with built-in driver when:

  • You're targeting clients who value quality and reliability over price
  • The project demands smooth dimming, flicker-free performance, and consistent light output
  • You want to minimize after-sales complaints and warranty claims
  • Your brand positioning is premium or mid-to-high end

Modules without built-in driver may work when:

  • Your client has a tight budget and basic on/off lighting is acceptable
  • The project is price-sensitive (e.g., mass-market residential, speculative developments)
  • Performance trade-offs like no dimming or basic light quality are understood and accepted
  • You're competing on lowest price in a tender or bulk supply deal

Both options have their place in the market. The key is knowing what you're offering and what your customer actually needs — and being transparent about the difference.

The Bottom Line

When a supplier quotes you a magnetic track module, ask one question: "Does this module have a built-in driver, or does it need an external one?"

If they can't answer clearly, that's a red flag. If they can, you now know exactly what you're comparing — and whether the price difference is justified by real hardware inside.
